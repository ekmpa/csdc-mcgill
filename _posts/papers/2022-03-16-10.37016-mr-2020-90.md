abstract_fr: This research examines how false beliefs shape perceptions of 
  government transparency in times of crisis. Measuring transparency perceptions
  using both closed- and open-ended questions drawn from a Canadian panel 
  survey, we show that individuals holding false beliefs about COVID-19 are more
  likely to have negative perceptions of government transparency. They also tend
  to rely on their false beliefs when asked to justify why they think 
  governments are not being transparent about the pandemic. Our findings suggest
  that the inability to successfully debunk misinformation could worsen 
  perceptions of government transparency, further eroding political support and 
  contributing to non-compliance with public health directives.
