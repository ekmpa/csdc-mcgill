abstract_fr: Online sex trafficking is on the rise and a majority of trafficking
  victims report being advertised online. The use of OnlyFans as a platform for 
  adult content is also increasing, with Twitter as its main advertising tool. 
  Furthermore, we know that traffickers usually work within a network and 
  control multiple victims. Consequently, we suspect that there may be networks 
  of traffickers promoting multiple OnlyFans accounts belonging to their 
  victims. To this end, we present the first study of OnlyFans advertisements on
  Twitter in the context of finding organized activities.
