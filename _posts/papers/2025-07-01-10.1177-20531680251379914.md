abstract_fr: Do different economic shocks favor different types of political 
  leadership? Using a survey experiment conducted on 3500 American respondents, 
  we offer causal evidence for an unexpected relation between different types of
  economic shocks and a demand for a radical political response. First, we show 
  that individuals believe politicians have a role in preventing layoffs due to 
  both offshoring and automation, compared to run-of-the-mill bankruptcies. 
  Second, we find strong evidence that economic shocks due to offshoring lead to
  greater demands for leaders who display authoritarian traits, with no 
  equivalent effect for layoffs due to automation. When presented with news of 
  an offshoring event, respondents favor leaders who claim to be more willing to
  flout the rule of law, to implement divisive policies, and to employ force. By
  contrast, traits commonly associated with populism, such as siding with “the 
  people” or preferring political outsiders, see no association with either 
  offshoring or automation shocks. Our findings support the view that some 
  economic shocks provide a greater opportunity for political candidates willing
  to turn to radical political solutions.
