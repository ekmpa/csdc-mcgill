abstract_fr: In recent years, increased interest has been paid to the effects of social
  diversity on the health and well-being of democratic politics in advanced, industrialised
  societies. Immigration from non-traditional source countries, whose populations
  are largely of non-European decent, is changing the face of European and North American
  societies. Popular commentary and academic research have recently raised concerns
  about how this changing demographic reality will impact citizens' politics. At the
  same time, advanced industrialised democracies continue to be divided by issues
  of class, ideology, religion and, in many states, language. How do various forms
  of diversity - both new and old - shape democratic politics?
