abstract_fr: We propose that major powers give foreign aid to developing 
  countries to facilitate politically costly economic reforms that preferential 
  trading agreements prescribe. Democratic developing countries (1) need 
  adjustment assistance more than autocracies and (2) can credibly commit to 
  using fungible revenue to compensate the domestic losers, so a side payment 
  for deeper reforms should only be available for democracies. A quantitative 
  test lends support to the theory. Fully democratic developing countries that 
  form a preferential trading agreement with the European Union or the United 
  States obtain a large increase in foreign aid in the short run. These results 
  imply that donors have used foreign aid to strengthen the effect of 
  preferential trading agreements on economic reforms.
