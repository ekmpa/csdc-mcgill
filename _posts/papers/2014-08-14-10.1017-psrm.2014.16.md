abstract_fr: The nonverbal display of confidence is strongly associated with 
  leadership and power. However, its importance for the persuasiveness of 
  campaign messages has not been explored. How important is showing confidence 
  for a political candidate’s ratings? How does confidence condition the effect 
  of the quality of a candidate’s arguments? This article addresses these 
  questions using an innovative experimental approach that makes it possible to 
  better isolate the impact of the candidate’s nonverbal confidence and the 
  quality of his message. While both of these aspects influence voters’ 
  perceptions of the candidate’s electability and qualifications, the nonverbal 
  dimension matters more when it comes to electability. This research 
  contributes to the study of nonverbal communication in elections by expanding 
  the focus of inquiry beyond the effect of pure emotions (happiness or anger) 
  and facial traits.
