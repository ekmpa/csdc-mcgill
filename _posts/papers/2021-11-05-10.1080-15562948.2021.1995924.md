abstract_fr: Although recent scholarship shows that restrictive visa policies curtail
  migration, research does not disaggregate policy effects within migration flows.
  We analyze Mexico-Canada migration when a travel visa was imposed in 2009 and removed
  in 2016. The imposition coincided with a dramatic decrease in travel and refugee
  claims. However, the number of Mexican immigrants grew, contrasting expectations
  of migration policy effectiveness research. We suggest that future research should
  disaggregate effects of policy changes, consider a state’s broader approach to migration
  policy, and examine how subnational programs and number of legal migration pathways
  influence trends of mixed migration flows.
