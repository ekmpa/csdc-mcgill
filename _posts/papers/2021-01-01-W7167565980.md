abstract_fr: Perceivers’ cognitive representations of faces may become associated
  with particular traits due to correlated face-trait experiences. To the extent that
  gender norms and stereotypes reinforce associations between gender and specific
  traits (e.g., associating facial femininity with warmth or masculinity with competence),
  we expect to see cultural differences in the conceptual space of trait impressions
  inferred from faces. We test this possibility, examining to what extent gender-related
  indices (e.g., gender inequality) in different cultures influence the way that people
  in those cultures form impressions of women and men.
