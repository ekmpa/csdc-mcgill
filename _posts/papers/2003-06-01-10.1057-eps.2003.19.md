abstract_fr: 'The routine finding of a positive relation between participation in
  voluntary associations and civic values and attitudes can be explained by two different
  causal mechanisms. On the one hand, some authors stress a process of self-selection:
  those who already have a more civic value pattern will be over-represented in voluntary
  associations, but their attitudes are not necessarily influenced by interaction
  within the associations (Stolle, 2001; Uslaner, 2002). Other authors emphasise socialisation
  effects: because of the interaction with like minded others, members of associations
  are imbued with more social and cooperative attitudes. In this view, associations
  function as ‘a learning school of democracy’ (Putnam, 1993; Hooghe, 2003). While
  the socialisation approach seems to dominate the social capital literature (cfr.
  Putnam, 2000, 2002), the empirical research currently available does not fully support
  the assumption that associational involvement has strong socialisation effects.
  Although people who are members of associations are usually more trusting and tolerant,
  so far no one has convincingly demonstrated that social participation experiences
  have a strong independent effect on civic attitudes.'
