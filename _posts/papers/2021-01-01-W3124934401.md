abstract_fr: This paper examines the impact of terrorism on voting behavior in the
  United States. We rely on an exhaustive list of terror attacks over the period 1970-2016
  and exploit the inherent randomness of the success or failure of terror attacks
  to identify the political impacts of terrorism. We first confirm that the success
  of terror attacks is plausibly random by showing that it is orthogonal to potential
  confounders. We then show that on average successful attacks have no effect on presidential
  and non-presidential elections. As a benchmark, we also rely on a more naïve identification
  strategy using all the counties not targeted by terrorists as a comparison group.
  We show that using this naïve identification strategy leads to strikingly different
  results overestimating the effect of terror attacks on voting behavior. Overall,
  our results indicate that terrorism has less of an influence on voters than is usually
  thought.
