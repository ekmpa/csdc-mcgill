abstract_fr: "Can international institutions help leaders commit economic reform?\
  \ In this article, we examine\r\nhow leaders use preferential trading agreements\
  \ with major powers (European Union and\r\nthe United States) to promote liberal\
  \ economic policies. We argue that under democratization,\r\nnew leaders benefit\
  \ the most from credible commitment. Using original data on treaty negotiations,\r\
  \nour empirical analysis shows that under democratization, leader change greatly\
  \ increases\r\nthe probability that the government of a developing country begins\
  \ treaty negotiations. We\r\nalso demonstrate that preferential trading agreements\
  \ are accompanied by liberalization in different\r\nsectors of the economy, and\
  \ this effect is most pronounced if it follows a leader change.\r\nThese findings\
  \ support the notion that international institutions enable credible commitment\
  \ to\r\neconomic reform."
