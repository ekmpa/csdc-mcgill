abstract_fr: This paper seeks to return scholarly attention to a core 
  intellectual divide between segmented and conventional (or neo‐)assimilation 
  approaches, doing so through a theoretical and empirical reconsideration of 
  contextual effects on second‐generation outcomes. We evaluate multiple 
  approaches to measuring receiving country contextual effects and measuring 
  their impact on the educational attainment of the children of immigrants. We 
  demonstrate that our proposed measures better predict second‐generation 
  educational attainment than prevailing approaches, enabling a multilevel 
  modeling strategy that accounts for the structure of immigrant families nested
  within different receiving contexts.
