abstract_fr: Few have asked about political implications of increasing income 
  inequalities in Canada. Over several generations, those in higher echelons 
  have enjoyed considerable growth, while those in lower tiers have seen no 
  growth, or worse, declines. This leads us to ask whether there also exists a 
  bifurcation of political attitudes, with those in the lower income tiers 
  showing more negative orientations compared to those who fare much better. 
  More precisely, we examine whether growing income inequality—mainly the 
  growing income gap—has any measurable effect on political attitudes. We 
  approach this study by incorporating econometric data from Statistics Canada 
  and survey data from the Canadian Election Study, spanning from the early 
  1990s to 2011.
