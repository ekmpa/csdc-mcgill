abstract_fr: This article looks at collective memory formation—the study of 
  monuments, memory, and public space—through a political science lens. An 
  explicit theoretical focus on power relations in "monumental politics" and a 
  methodological approach featuring large-N comparative analysis are combined to
  examine the process of monument creation, destruction, and alteration. Using a
  new database on monuments in 26 post-communist states over a 25-year period, 
  patterns of monument transformation are identified, and official and private 
  efforts to transform monuments are systematically compared across different 
  regime types. The complex implications of private provision and alteration of 
  monuments is also discussed.
