abstract_fr: This study compared antiretroviral therapy (ART) persistence among 
  people with HIV (PWH) in the United States. This was a retrospective analysis 
  of Optum Research Database claims from PWH with commercial insurance or 
  Medicare Advantage with Part D coverage who switched to or restarted 
  bictegravir (B)/emtricitabine (F)/tenofovir alafenamide (TAF), dolutegravir 
  (DTG)/lamivudine (3TC), DTG/abacavir (ABC)/3TC, DTG+F/TAF, DTG+F/tenofovir 
  disoproxil fumarate (TDF), or cabotegravir+rilpivirine (CAB+RPV) from 
  31/7/2017–30/11/2023. After inverse probability treatment weighting of 
  baseline characteristics, nonpersistence (ie, discontinuation, switch, add-on,
  or death) was evaluated for each regimen using Kaplan-Meier analyses and 
  adjusted Cox proportional hazards models. 14,826 treatment-experienced PWH 
  were included (mean [SD] age, 52.7 [13.0] years; 80.4% male; 41.4% on Medicare
  Advantage). The proportion who remained persistent at 12 months was 
  significantly greater for B/F/TAF (81.7%) compared with DTG/3TC (78.3%), 
  DTG/ABC/3TC (67.6%), DTG+F/TAF (64.5%), and DTG+F/TDF (39.8%; all <i>p</i> 
  &lt; 0.05), but not CAB+RPV (75.5%; <i>p</i> = 0.063). Compared with B/F/TAF, 
  risk of nonpersistence was greater for DTG/ABC/3TC, DTG+F/TAF, and DTG+F/TDF, 
  with an adjusted HR (95% CI) of 1.74 (1.58–1.92), 1.99 (1.80–2.20), and 5.01 
  (4.15–6.06), respectively (all <i>p</i> &lt; 0.001). Generally similar trends 
  were observed among those on Medicare Advantage. Treatment-experienced PWH who
  received B/F/TAF had a high likelihood of persistence. People with HIV (PWH) 
  must consistently take medicine for successful HIV treatment. This study used 
  health insurance claims data to look at how long PWH remained on their 
  medicine before changing to a new medicine or stopping it altogether. In 
  total, 14,826 PWH in the United States with commercial insurance or Medicare 
  Advantage with Part D coverage and who were taking 1 of 6 different HIV 
  medicines were included in the study. PWH who were taking 
  bictegravir/emtricitabine/tenofovir alafenamide were more likely to stay on 
  their original medicine compared with PWH who were taking 
  dolutegravir/abacavir/lamivudine, dolutegravir + emtricitabine/tenofovir 
  alafenamide, or dolutegravir + emtricitabine/tenofovir disoproxil fumarate. 
  These findings provide a better understanding of how long PWH stay on certain 
  medicines, which can help health care providers decide which medicine to 
  prescribe their patients.
