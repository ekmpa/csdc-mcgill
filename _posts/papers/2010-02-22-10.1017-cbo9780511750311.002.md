abstract_fr: "By the end of the twentieth century, most Latin American countries could
  look back on their longest uninterrupted and, as of this writing, still open-ended
  period of political democracy. No country except Chile and Uruguay had enjoyed a
  longer run of full or even constrained democratic competition in any previous era.
  Before 1980, only five Latin American countries had achieved episodes of more than
  twelve continuous years of democratic or semidemocratic rule, but by 1999 that threshold
  had been crossed by no fewer than six additional countries. By the turn of the millennium,
  four of the five countries with comparatively durable democratic experiences before
  1970 had once again accumulated a record of more than twelve years of continuous
  democracy since their most recent democratic transitions. Consequently, Latin America
  stands out as the most uniformly democratic region on earth behind the established
