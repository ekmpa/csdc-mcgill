abstract_fr: Abstract With case studies of Chile and Colombia, this chapter 
  shows that reform demand induces preferential trading agreement (PTA) 
  negotiations even in the absence of democratic transition. In both cases, the 
  combination of demand for liberalization and a new leader’s political weakness
  led to PTA negotiations, and the agreement facilitated reform. Chile’s 
  left-wing government was able to build political support for various reforms, 
  such as privatization, and enhance their credibility by forming trade treaties
  with the EU and the US. In Colombia, a PTA with the US contributed to economic
  reform and growth in a politically unstable environment characterized by civil
  conflict.
