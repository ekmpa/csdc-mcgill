abstract_fr: Founder-centric firms developed as the necessary consequence of 
  their founders' pursuit of independence. The leaders of those firms today have
  enjoyed great success as a result of their initial entrepreneurial risk taking
  and are understandably reluctant to relinquish authority. But that 
  founder-centric model now presents obstacles to effective transitions and 
  continued client service excellence. Successors can never truly share the 
  burdens and benefits of this foundational experience and can never be members 
  of this fraternity of founders. Instead, successors must battle the flawed 
  beliefs many founders share. All parties to the transition imperative lack any
  significant experience. It's usually a first-and-only event, but with huge 
  impacts for clients, staff, founders, and successors. Still, founders and 
  successors can unite in their common desire to create something even better 
  for clients
