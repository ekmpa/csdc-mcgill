abstract_fr: Abstract This chapter applies the general argument to the case of 
  preferential trading agreements (PTAs). First, the chapter argues that a 
  leader’s willingness to implement economic reforms depends on recent 
  democratization, while the leader’s ability to do so depends on how long she 
  has been in power. These observable factors allow us to conduct a statistical 
  test of the argument that demand for reform (democratization), combined with 
  political impediments to implementation (new leader), drive leaders in 
  developing countries to engage in PTA negotiations with the EU and the US. 
  Next, the chapter explains why PTAs—especially with the EU or the US—have 
  unique potential to help leaders cut the Gordian knot of economic reform. For 
  one, these PTAs create enhanced market access for exporters. If economic 
  reform is a precondition for market access, exporters support the agreement. 
  Similarly, these PTAs increase foreign direct investment. This benefits 
  capital owners.
