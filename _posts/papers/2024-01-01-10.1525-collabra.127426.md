abstract_fr: 'Intergroup contact is robustly associated with lower prejudice. However,
  nearly all contact research to date has examined a narrow range of groups, raising
  the possibility that its effects are restricted to only some types of groups. Across
  two studies (total N = 2163), we tested whether the contact-prejudice relationship
  was identical for a broad sample of groups (k = 80), whether group properties moderated
  this relationship, and whether the relationship depended on the type of intergroup
  contact. Results indicate that contact was consistently associated with lower prejudice
  across all groups, group properties, and measures of contact. Together, our results
  broadly support intergroup contact theory: contact is negatively associated with
  prejudice across groups with a wide array of distinct group properties.'
