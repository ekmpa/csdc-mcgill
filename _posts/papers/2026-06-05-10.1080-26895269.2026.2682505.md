abstract_fr: 'Background Transgender and gender diverse (TGD) adolescents experience
  disproportionate rates of adversity, risk behaviors, and suicidality. However, the
  complex, interdependent pathways linking these domains remain poorly understood,
  largely due to the limitations of traditional unidirectional modeling. This study
  applies Bayesian Ising model analysis to map the symptom topology of TGD Adolescents
  and identify specific psychopathological mechanisms driven by compounding minority
  stress.Methods Using data from the 2023 Youth Risk Behavior Survey, I estimated
  a 23-node Bayesian Ising model on a propensity-score-matched sample of 401 TGD and
  401 cisgender adolescents. Nodes were grouped into adversity/trauma, health-risk
  behaviors, and psychological distress. I computed expected influence and bridge
  expected influence with 95% highest density intervals to identify network hubs and
  cross-cluster bridges.Results Interpersonal abuse and household dysfunction emerged
  as primary structural hubs. A dual-pathway structure mediated the adversity-to-suicidality
  trajectory: an affective conduit via sadness/hopelessness and a behavioral conduit
  via substance use (notably early alcohol initiation). The TGD network exhibited
  denser intra-cluster connectivity than the matched cisgender network. Furthermore,
  structural topology varied significantly by sex assigned at birth: substance use
  functioned as the dominant bridge for Adolescents assigned female at birth, whereas
  emotional abuse and household violence anchored the network for Adolescents assigned
  male at birth.Conclusions The psychopathological network of TGD Adolescents is driven
  by interpersonal abuse and bridged to suicidality through distinct affective and
  behavioral mechanisms. Findings highlight the necessity of replacing one-size-fits-all
  approaches with targeted public health interventions sensitive to specific bridging
  pathways and intra-group structural heterogeneities.'
