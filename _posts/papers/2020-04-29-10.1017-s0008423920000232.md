abstract_fr: Abstract One reform considered for increasing voter turnout rates 
  is to lower the voting age to 16 years old. Advocates of such a reform argue 
  that young people would vote for the first time while they are still in school
  and living with their parents, which would provide a social context that is 
  supportive of their electoral participation. However, opponents argue that 16-
  and 17-year-olds are not mature enough to take part in elections. Using data 
  from a 2018 Quebec election survey that included a subsample of individuals 
  aged 16 and 17, this study provides mixed evidence for both arguments.
