abstract_fr: This chapter examines the capacity of voluntary associations to 
  facilitate generalized trust, since social capital theorists assume the 
  existence of this capacity. With voluntary associations as with other 
  potential sources for generalized trust, the task is to specify the mechanisms
  and aspects of the interactions that are either promoting or inhibiting for 
  generalized trust. Social capital theory has fleshed out a few criteria for 
  the inclusion or exclusion of certain types of social interaction productive 
  of social capital; these criteria serve as the foundations for the choice of 
  hypotheses. Values of inclusion/ exclusion filtered through the educational 
  system as well as the set of welfare institutions, the duration of democratic 
  experiences and other differences between Germany, Sweden and the United 
  States should have an impact on the development of trust between people. 
  Membership of voluntary associations should increase face-to-face interactions
  between people and create a setting for the development of trust.
