abstract_fr: 'The present research informs a contemporary and unsettled debate in
  research on prejudice: Does implicit prejudice better reflect influences that originate
  in the individual or the environment in which they live? To examine this question,
  we quantified individual- and macro-level variation in implicit prejudice, taking
  a global (N=34 countries), cross-cultural approach. We further investigated the
  relative contribution of these factors for Race, Age, Body weight, Skin-tone, and
  Sexuality attitudes. Moreover, we compared these patterns between implicit and explicit
  prejudice. We consistently found that individual differences accounted for most
  variance in both implicit and explicit prejudice. Macro-level variation played a
  small but not negligible role. In exploring the correlates of this macro-level variation,
  we found that a country’s endorsement of cultural values, but not its level of economic
  inequality, predicted this variance. These findings are consistent with the view
  that individual differences more so than environments underlie implicit (and explicit)
  prejudice.'
