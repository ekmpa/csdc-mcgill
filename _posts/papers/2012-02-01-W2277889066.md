abstract_fr: How do domestic politics influence the formation of international institutions,
  and what are the effects of international institutions on domestic politics? In
  this policy paper, we examine how leaders use preferential trading agreements (PTAs)
  with the European Union and the United States to promote liberal economic policies.
  We argue that under democratization, new leaders would benefit the most from credible
  commitment and side payments to compensate vulnerable domestic constituencies for
  their losses. Our empirical analysis shows that under democratization, leader change
  greatly increases the probability that the government of a developing country begins
  treaty negotiations. We also demonstrate that PTAs induce liberalization in different
  sectors of the economy.
