abstract_fr: What explains subnational policy choices over tax cut after decentralization?
  We test two different explanations in the context of the 2002 tax reform in Russia.
  A popular strand of literature suggests that decentralization induces more regional
  competition over investment, motivating subnational tax cuts. A second body of literature
  suggests that personal business interests of regional governors can account for
  their different policy choices. Governors with personal business ties refrain from
  tax cuts because they increase market competition. We find no support for the regional
  competition hypothesis, but strong statistical evidence for the business connection
  hypothesis. Our findings have important implications for research on fiscal decentralization
  and on the connections between business interests of leaders and their policy choices.
