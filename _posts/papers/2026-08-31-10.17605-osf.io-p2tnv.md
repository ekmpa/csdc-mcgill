abstract_fr: This study is a direct replication of our initial investigation of racial
  bias in online dating decisions. The project examines whether the individual-difference
  variables identified as predictors of discriminatory dating decisions in the original
  study reliably predict the same decisions in an independent sample. Participants
  will complete the same online dating task and self-report measures used in the original
  study. During the dating task, participants will view a series of profiles and indicate
  whether they would swipe right or left on each profile. The profiles will vary in
  the perceived racial group of the person depicted, allowing us to assess participants’
  dating decisions toward racial ingroup and outgroup targets. In the original study,
  an elastic net regression model was used to identify a reduced set of predictors
  associated with racial bias in dating decisions. The present replication will retain
  only the predictors selected by that model. Rather than conducting another data-driven
  variable-selection procedure, we will use the retained predictors as prespecified
  confirmatory variables and estimate their regression coefficients in the new sample.
  This approach will allow us to evaluate the direction, magnitude, and uncertainty
  of each predictor’s association with dating decisions and determine whether the
  predictive patterns observed in the original study are reproducible. Hence, the
  principal objective is to obtain unbiased estimates and uncertainty intervals for
  the prespecified predictor coefficients in an independent sample, rather than to
  select additional predictors or maximize predictive performance. Overall, this replication
  will provide evidence regarding the robustness and generalizability of the predictors
  identified in the original elastic net model. It will also help distinguish predictors
  that show stable associations with racial bias in dating decisions from those whose
  original effects may have been sample-specific.
