abstract_fr: Programmatic party systems develop during extended periods of 
  adaptive political learning, catalyzed by historical episodes in which 
  momentous societal conflicts crystallize around alternative modes of 
  political-economic domination. When large constituencies and their political 
  representatives struggle for the adoption of far-reaching political-economic 
  arrangements, and when they have the opportunity to play electoral politics 
  repeatedly, then advocates of change and their antagonists may coordinate 
  around rival programmatic party visions. These alternatives become 
  progressively more structured through a process of building lasting party 
  organizations and by locking in social and economic policies and institutions 
  that bind electoral constituencies to specific partisan alternatives. The 
  development of such divides may extend over decades, even though it may erupt 
  in profound economic and political crises and violent domestic or external 
  conflicts.
