abstract_fr: Using a comprehensive, micro-scale dataset of Canadian Federal election
  returns, this chapter analyzes the geographic distribution of party support over
  the last two decades. The analysis is inspired by Agnew long-standing attention
  to scale and context in electoral geography. Canada’s multi-party system permits
  a nuanced analysis of political shifts because the ideological spectrum is divided
  among six “major” parties. While many advanced industrial democracies have experienced
  ideological polarization during this period, there is little evidence of significant
  geographical sorting of Federal party support at the micro-scale between 2006 and
  2021. The results show that Conservative Party voters are associated with less politically
  diverse areas and that the party’s share of support comes disproportionally from
  such areas, but this pattern is likely explained by the Canadian party system. Three
  parties – the Liberals, New Democratic Party, and Greens – compete for left-leaning
  voters, while the Conservative Party is the only major party on the right since
  its consolidation in 2003. The separatist Bloc Québécois shows the strongest association
  with highly diverse areas, reflecting how its appeal cuts across the orthodox left-right
  political spectrum.
