abstract_fr: Despite the prevalence of recent success in learning from static 
  graphs, learning from time-evolving graphs remains an open challenge. In this 
  work, we design new, more stringent evaluation procedures for link prediction 
  specific to dynamic graphs, which reflect real-world considerations, to better
  compare the strengths and weaknesses of methods. First, we create two 
  visualization techniques to understand the reoccurring patterns of edges over 
  time and show that many edges reoccur at later time steps. Based on this 
  observation, we propose a pure memorization baseline called EdgeBank. EdgeBank
  achieves surprisingly strong performance across multiple settings because easy
  negative edges are often used in the current evaluation setting. To evaluate 
  against more difficult negative edges, we introduce two more challenging 
  negative sampling strategies that improve robustness and better match 
  real-world applications. Lastly, we introduce six new dynamic graph datasets 
  from a diverse set of domains missing from current benchmarks, providing new 
  challenges and opportunities for future research. Our code repository is 
  accessible at https://github.com/fpour/DGB.git.
