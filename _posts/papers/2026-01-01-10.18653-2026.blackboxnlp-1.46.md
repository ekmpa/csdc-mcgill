abstract_fr: Large language models (LLMs) are used as agents in social-media simulations,
  where behavioral fidelity may depend on preserving the affective intensity of real
  discourse.Using 3,692 unique Bluesky posts expanded into 14,768 activation records,
  we extract mildstrong affect directions in Qwen2.5 and Llama-3.1 Base and instruction-tuned
  checkpoints.Measured separation and reciprocal transfer to non-political hostility
  support a broader hostility-related feature rather than a uniquely partisan axis.In
  a six-prompt steering probe, checkpoints differ in their labeled hostile expression,
  refusal/deflection, and neutralization.Refusal/deflection includes substantive answers
  with disclaimers.Same-layer comparisons distinguish the hostility directions from
  a measured harmful-request contrast; removing that component changes behavioral
  counts non-uniformly and does not consistently return them to baseline.These descriptive
  results illustrate a representation-expression gap and motivate downstream fidelity
  audits.
