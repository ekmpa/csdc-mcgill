abstract_fr: Abstract A person's impression of another depends upon three 
  sources of variance. The characteristics of the target, the characteristics of
  the perceiver, and the interplay between the two. Researchers have dedicated 
  different amounts of study to these three sources of variance, and therefore 
  they differ in how well they are understood. The present work will first 
  review the portions of the face impression process that are understood well, 
  then identify and discuss portions of the process less well understood. We 
  will then question to what extent the current state of knowledge will 
  generalize to novel targets and populations. Finally, we will review several 
  modeling approaches that can accommodate relatively unexamined yet important 
  sources of variance in impression formation, suggesting a clear path forward 
  toward a comprehensive understanding of face impressions.
