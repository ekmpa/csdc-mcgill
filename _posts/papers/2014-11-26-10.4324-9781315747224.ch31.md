abstract_fr: "Increasing levels of ethnic and racial diversity in industrialized democracies
  have given \nrise to growing concerns about community and social cohesion in Europe
  and North America. \nThere are worries that ethnic diversity may threaten the ties
  that bind civil society together \n(Putnam 2007). Sceptics of diversity seem to
  believe that diverse societies create problems. \nAccordingly, the assumption is
  that they are more likely to breed ethnic confl ict, less likely to \ndevelop into
  stable democracies, less likely to enact a solidaristic social welfare system and
  less \nlikely to foster widespread generalized trust. While some of these earlier
  concerns have been \nrefuted, pessimism continues to linger, especially within the
  broader public debate. Indeed, the \nacceptance of cultural diversity is relatively
  low across Europe, and the rise of far-right parties in \nsome European countries
  has been partly fuelled by widespread anti-immigrant sentiment \n(Sniderman et al.
  2000)."
