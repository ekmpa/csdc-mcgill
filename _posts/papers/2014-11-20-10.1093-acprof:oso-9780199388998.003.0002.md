abstract_fr: 'Abstract This chapter presents the general argument of the book. It
  begins by arguing that economic reform presents two major problems: domestic political
  opposition and lack of credibility. The chapter then explains why international
  institutions can help leaders solve these problems. First, international institutions
  enhance the credibility of commitment. Second, international institutions create
  opportunities, such as enhanced market access and foreign aid, that mobilize winners
  and compensate losers. The chapter concludes with an explanation for why international
  institutions with major power members are particularly valuable. The reason is that
  major powers have the resources and power to enforce commitments and offer valuable
  opportunities to developing countries, while the global economic interests of these
  powers ensure that they also have an interest in treaty cooperation.'
