abstract_fr: The coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) pandemic has quickly become
  a global\npublic health crisis unseen in recent years. It is known that the 
  structure of\nthe human contact network plays an important role in the spread 
  of\ntransmissible diseases. In this work, we study a structure aware model 
  of\nCOVID-19 CGEM. This model becomes similar to the classical 
  compartment-based\nmodels in epidemiology if we assume the contact network is 
  a Erdos-Renyi (ER)\ngraph, i.e. everyone comes into contact with everyone else
  with the same\nprobability. In contrast, CGEM is more expressive and allows 
  for plugging in\nthe actual contact networks, or more realistic proxies for 
  it. Moreover, CGEM\nenables more precise modelling of enforcing and releasing 
  different\nnon-pharmaceutical intervention (NPI) strategies. Through a set of 
  extensive\nexperiments, we demonstrate significant differences between the 
  epidemic curves\nwhen assuming different underlying structures. More 
  specifically we demonstrate\nthat the compartment-based models are 
  overestimating the spread of the\ninfection by a factor of 3, and under some 
  realistic assumptions on the\ncompliance factor, underestimating the 
  effectiveness of some of NPIs,\nmischaracterizing others (e.g. predicting a 
  later peak), and underestimating\nthe scale of the second peak after 
  reopening.\n
