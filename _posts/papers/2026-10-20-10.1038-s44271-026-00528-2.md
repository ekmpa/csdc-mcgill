abstract_fr: 'Brain-based predictive models offer a promising approach to understanding
  individual differences in cognitive functioning across the lifespan. This review
  synthesizes current literature on brain-based cognitive predictions using large-scale
  datasets, examining how methodological choices and data-related factors impact model
  performance. We find that brain-based predictive models demonstrate relatively consistent,
  albeit wide ranging performance across the lifespan, explaining approximately 10-40%
  of variance in cognitive abilities. Key findings include: (1) models perform better
  when predicting general cognitive ability compared to specific functions; (2) crystallized
  abilities are more predictable than fluid abilities; (3) task-based functional connectivity
  generally outperforms resting-state approaches in younger populations, while structural
  measures become superior predictors in older adults; (4) multimodal approaches using
  hierarchical stacked models optimize prediction accuracy; and (5) elastic net and
  ridge regression provide optimal balance between predictive performance and computational
  efficiency. In summary, we propose methodological advances to improve the precision
  and utility of brain-based cognitive predictions.'
