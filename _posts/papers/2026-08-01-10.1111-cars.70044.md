abstract_fr: The measures to limit COVID-19 and the pandemic had deleterious effects
  on social cohesion. We propose that perceived clarity and coherence of governmental
  measures and perceived majority support of these measures together create a normative
  context that shapes social cohesion. Using a representative sample of adult Canadians
  (N = 1180) and a longitudinal design, we aim to (1) identify profiles of perceived
  clarity, coherence and majority support of governmental measures at three time points
  during the first year of the pandemic and (2) determine if the dynamics identified
  are associated with attitudes and behaviours capturing social cohesion at a later
  time. Results showed that two profiles for perceived clarity/coherence and for perceived
  majority support best described our data. For both profiles, about 75% of the sample
  was classified in a high-level decreasing profile and 25% in a low-level decreasing
  profile. The identified profiles were associated with social cohesion months later.
  Our results provide insights that could be used by governments to better plan the
  message sent to the population during a future crisis to maximize social cohesion.
