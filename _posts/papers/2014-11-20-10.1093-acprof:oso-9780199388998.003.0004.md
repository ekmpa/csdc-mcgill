abstract_fr: Abstract This chapter shows that EU and US preferential trading 
  agreements (PTAs) contain a wide range of provisions that prescribe deep 
  economic reforms, often unrelated to trade. The chapter also shows that this 
  is not true of other preferential trading agreements, with the partial 
  exception of Japan. To illustrate the contrast, the chapter also considers 
  recent Chinese trade agreements. The analysis in this chapter is based on 
  original data collected by the authors. The main treaty provisions that the 
  chapter considers relate to intellectual property rights, liberalization in 
  services, foreign direct investment, competition policy, and government 
  procurement.
