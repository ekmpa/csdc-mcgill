abstract_fr: Those advocating the removal of US Confederate monuments have 
  generally relied on the claim that because the ideas these monuments represent
  (i.e. White supremacy) have no legitimate place in political discourse, the 
  monuments should be removed from public space. While we share this normative 
  position, experiences while teaching our interdisciplinary undergraduate 
  course on Memory, Place, and Power forced us to interrogate our reflexive 
  desire to ‘take ’em down’. We learned that as scholars and practitioners, we 
  must not only better explain and defend the nature of the ‘forgetting’ that 
  happens when we remove Confederate monuments but also put our discussion of 
  their fate into a broader international context, one that embraces a range of 
  alternatives beyond the stark choice of removal versus retention.
