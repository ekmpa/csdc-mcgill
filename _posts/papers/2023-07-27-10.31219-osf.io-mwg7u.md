abstract_fr: "How do the receipt and perceived quality of election information impact
  citizens' forecasts and interpretations of actual election results? I hypothesize
  that three factors shape how citizensemploy electoral information in developing
  democracies: the distribution of their beliefs prior to receiving information, the
  perceived quality of this information, and how actual election results align with
  their expectations. To test my theory, I develop anew elicitation technique to measure
  subjects' distribution of expectations pre and post-treatment. I employ this technique
  in a lab-in-the-fieldexperiment in the country of Georgia, where I vary the identity
  of the endorser of poll predictions derived from a pre-election survey I conducted
  and used as the information treatment. I find the endorser of the information is
  the strongest predictor of whether subjects change the distribution of their beliefs.
  I also propose uses of my technique beyond the developing democracy context."
