abstract_fr: "Does fine-tuning on a user's social-media history make an LLM a better\
  \ simulator of that user?We study this question using QLoRA, conditioning models\
  \ on a user's chronological action history and a reply thread before generating\
  \ that user's reply.Across 12 matched baseline-QLoRA pairs from four model families\
  \ (0.8B-32B), plus a baseline-only 122B model, LLM-as-judge believability evaluations\
  \ show that QLoRA improves several smaller models, has mixed effects on stronger\
  \ models, and does not lift a sub-10B model into the strongest large-model tier.However,\
  \ a realversus-generated classifier gives a different result: QLoRA makes Qwen,\
  \ OLMo, and Llama replies much less detectable, while Gemma remains readily detectable.Contextual\
  \ believability and detector AUC are uncorrelated across systems.We further explore\
  \ LLM evaluations: strong LLM judges agree on broad quality tiers, and a blind 200-comparison\
  \ audit finds humanjudge agreement of 66% when three strong judges agree and 47%\
  \ when they split.The measured effect of QLoRA therefore depends on the evaluation\
  \ axis."
