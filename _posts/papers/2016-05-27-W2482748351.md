abstract_fr: The question of state capacity and how to strengthen it remains a challenge
  as the underlying incentives of the state, its citizens, and its agents align in
  some areas of state activity and diverge in others. This paper lays out a framework
  for state capacity around the extractive, regulatory and productive domains that
  define the relationships between rulers and their citizens, as well as the bureaucrats
  in between. We argue that these three domains loosely correspond to three theoretical
  traditions of state capacity a liberal one, a developmental one and one on governance.
  We then discuss the makings of a good bureaucracy, highlighting the need to better
  understand how incentives affect performance. We close with a call to focus on non-state
  sources of state capacity such as the norms of compliance associated with different
  cultural traditions and the informal institutions that promote monitoring and feedback
