abstract_fr: In the United States, prospective adoptive parents often express 
  preferences related to race. In two studies, we examined whether implicit 
  racial bias against Black people may contribute to disparities in much less 
  willingness to adopt Black children. The first study (N = 510) assessed 
  individuals’ implicit racial bias and their willingness to adopt a Black 
  child. The second study (N = 2,001,652) used U.S. state-level implicit racial 
  bias to predict adoption rates of Black foster children in each U.S. state. 
  Greater implicit racial bias predicted less willingness to adopt Black 
  children and less frequent adoptions of Black foster children. Implicit bias 
  contributed to these disparities above and beyond explicit bias, with implicit
  bias having a 43% larger effect size than explicit bias on willingness to 
  adopt a Black child. These are the first findings to demonstrate the role 
  implicit bias plays in explaining large disparities between Americans’ 
  willingness to adopt Black and White children.
