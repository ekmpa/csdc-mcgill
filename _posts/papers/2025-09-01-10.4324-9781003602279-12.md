abstract_fr: The existence and persistence of nationalist movements among 
  substate nations can have various explanations, one of which is related to 
  generations—how they were socialized to politics in distinct societal 
  contexts, and how older generations are replaced by younger ones. Hence, to 
  understand better the evolution of nationalism in Quebec, this study draws on 
  the last six Quebec Election Studies (2007–22) and uses an Age–Period–Cohort 
  model to examine the relationship between generational groups and various 
  indicators of nationalism. The results reveal that there is indeed a 
  generational story to tell about nationalism in Quebec. The generation of the 
  Baby Boomers is particularly distinct from other generations, in terms of its 
  attachment to Quebec, its support for Quebec’s independence and its support of
  the Parti Québécois; whereas the Millennials are more supportive of Québec 
  Solidaire and GenXers are more likely to support the Coalition Avenir Québec. 
  There are currently distinct “nationalist generations” that cohabitate in 
  Quebec.
