abstract_fr: The current research examines whether images presented alongside 
  online articles might systematically vary in how political targets are 
  presented. We focus our analyses on how presidents who either share or do not 
  share political orientations with Internet media outlets are portrayed along 
  the dimensions of warmth and competence, qualities highly prized in leadership
  and effectively conveyed in facial images. Four hundred twenty-two images from
  five online media outlets were coded for warmth and com-petence. Media outlets
  sharing political orientations with a target portrayed them as more warm and 
  competent than targets of another orientation. The viability of this effect as
  a potential mechanism to influence readers of online articles is discussed.
