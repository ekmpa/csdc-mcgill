abstract_fr: 'How does trade affect the design of&#13;\n preferential trade agreements
  (PTAs)? What is the role of&#13;\n global value chains (GVCs)? The authors answer
  these&#13;\n questions by empirically investigating the causal impact of&#13;\n
  gross and value-added trade on the depth of PTAs. To solve&#13;\n the critical issue
  of endogeneity of trade flows for trade&#13;\n policy, the identification strategy
  exploits a recent&#13;\n transportation shock: the sharp increase in the maximum
  size&#13;\n of container ships, which has more than tripled between 1995&#13;\n
  and 2007. The key variation in our instrument hinges on the&#13;\n fact that only
  deep-water ports can accommodate new larger&#13;\n ships. The strategy is flexible
  enough to generate&#13;\n excludable instruments for different value-added components&#13;\n
  of exports. This allows us to assess how the design (depth)&#13;\n of PTAs is affected
  not only by gross exports but more&#13;\n specifically by GVC-trade as captured
  by indicators of trade&#13;\n in domestic and foreign value added. The authors find
  that&#13;\n trade occurring through GVCs increases the probability of&#13;\n forming
  deep PTAs, i.e., agreements that include provisions&#13;\n that go beyond the coverage
  of the WTO. These GVC-trade&#13;\n effects are larger than those of gross exports,
  which&#13;\n include flows that are unrelated to GVCs. The results&#13;\n indicate
  that GVCs are one important driver of deep&#13;\n preferential liberalization.'
