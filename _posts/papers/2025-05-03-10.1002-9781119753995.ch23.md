abstract_fr: Nation refers to a specific kind of political community, bound by 
  ancestry, culture, and/or civic values, that emerged in its modern form in 
  seventeenth- to nineteenth-century Europe. While distinct from the state, the 
  nation serves as the most potent form of political solidarity in the 
  contemporary world, with the global political structure organized around the 
  idea of national self-determination and the nation-state. Nationalism refers 
  to any political project that seeks to create, cultivate, or reinforce a sense
  of national identity to further a political goal, most typically the formation
  or consolidation of a nation-state. Nation-building is the process for doing 
  so. Contemporary scholarship generally treats nations as modern inventions, in
  contrast to the traditional (primordialist) approaches that assert that they 
  represented ancient, unchanging populations. Various forms of nationalism 
  exist, ranging from relatively exclusionary ethnic nationalism to relatively 
  inclusive civic nationalism, but most movements are a mix of the two 
  principles.
