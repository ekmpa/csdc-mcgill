abstract_fr: 'Classic social psychology models of impression formation have primarily
  focused on facial attributes, often overlooking contextual cues such as bodies and
  clothing. This study examined how body and dress attributes (BDA) contribute to
  impression formation relative to facial cues. A total of 3,594 participants rated
  images of individuals under three conditions: face only, body only, or face and
  body combined. Participants evaluated targets on status, wealth, effort, dominance,
  trustworthiness, and attractiveness. Linear regression analyses were conducted to
  examine how face-only and body-only ratings predicted impressions of the combined
  stimuli. Results indicated that both facial and bodily cues significantly contributed
  to impressions across all dimensions. However, bodily cues showed larger standardized
  coefficients for status, wealth, effort, dominance, and trustworthiness, whereas
  facial cues were more strongly associated with attractiveness. These findings highlight
  the importance of considering body and dress attributes alongside facial information
  when studying impression formation in more ecologically valid contexts.'
