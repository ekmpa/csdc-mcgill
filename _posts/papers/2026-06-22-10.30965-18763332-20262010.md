abstract_fr: Abstract Despite substantial external financial inflows over the past
  decades, the Western Balkan (WB) countries continue to lag behind the European Union
  (EU) in economic convergence. Using a newly compiled dataset covering EU transfers,
  bilateral assistance, foreign direct investment, and remittances, this article compares
  the WB experience with that of Central Eastern Europe and the Baltics (CEEB). The
  findings challenge the common view that the WB’s slow growth is primarily due to
  insufficient external finance. In per capita terms, WB countries have received volumes
  of external funding comparable to – or in some cases exceeding – those provided
  to CEEB during their pre-accession period. What proved decisive for CEEB-EU convergence
  was not pre-accession funding, but different EU instruments and the substantial
  surge of financial resources after accession. The scale, timing, and effectiveness
  of post-accession EU financial instruments provided a major boost to growth and
  structural transformation. In contrast, persistent institutional weaknesses and
  governance deficits in the WB have significantly limited the impact of external
  finance. The analysis suggests that increasing EU funding alone will not accelerate
  convergence unless accompanied by stronger conditionality and reforms targeting
  the region’s structural governance challenges.
