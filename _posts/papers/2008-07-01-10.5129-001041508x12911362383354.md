  2 The purpose of this article is to present an alternative theory on the 
  generation of social capital. In the discussion about the sources of social 
  capital it has been stressed that generalized trust is built up by the 
  citizens themselves through a culture that permeates the networks and 
  organizations of civil society. Since this approach has run into conceptual 
  problems and has produced only mixed empirical evidence, we like to highlight 
  instead how social capital is embedded in and linked to formal political and 
  legal institutions. Not all political institutions matter equally, however. In
  fact, we argue that trust thrives most in societies with effective, impartial 
  and fair street-level bureaucracies. The article presents the causal mechanism
  between these institutional characteristics and generalized trust, and 
  illustrates its validity in a cross-national context.
