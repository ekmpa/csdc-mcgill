abstract_fr: 'Research on adverse childhood experiences (ACEs) is dominated by a cumulative
  risk model demonstrating a "dose-response" relationship between the quantity of
  adversities and negative outcomes. This paradigm, resting on a "homogeneity assumption,"
  obscures the heterogeneity, internal structure, and social stratification of ACEs,
  failing to explore which adversities and what combinations matter most. This paper
  introduces a relational, Bourdieusian perspective, conceptualizing ACEs as a multidimensional
  "space of adversity." Using multiple correspondence analysis (MCA) on data from
  the 2023 National Youth Risk Behavior Surveillance System (N = 13,217), I map the
  topology of 13 ACEs. This map is then used to situate key social positions (e.g.,
  race/ethnicity, sexual/gender identity) and health/behavioral outcomes (e.g., substance
  use, suicidality) within the emergent structure. Findings reveal the "space of adversity"
  is structured by three orthogonal dimensions: (1) General Burden of Adversity, (2)
  Locus of Adversity, and (3) Internal Nature of Adversity. This structure is highly
  stratified: Sexual and gender minority adolescent are located within an "External
  Social Harm" cluster. Furthermore, specific consequences correspond to distinct
  regions: substance use aligns with the "Family Dysfunction" cluster, whereas suicidality
  aligns with the "External Social Harm" cluster. This study challenges the "homogeneity
  assumption" by demonstrating that not all ACEs are equivalent; their meaning is
  structural and relational. By visualizing the topology of adversity, this research
  provides a new analytical framework for trauma research and offers crucial evidence
  for designing targeted interventions that address the specific adversity ecologies
  faced by vulnerable populations.'
