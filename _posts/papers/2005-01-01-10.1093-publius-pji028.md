abstract_fr: Are federal incumbents punished for national and/or provincial 
  economic performance, and are provincial incumbents held accountable for the 
  state of the provincial and/or national economy? Using a pooled 
  cross-sectional time-series analysis of electoral results and macroeconomic 
  data for 1953–2001, this article explores the extent to which provincial and 
  federal incumbents in Canadian elections are affected more by national or 
  provincial economic conditions. The results of the analysis suggest that 
  federal incumbents would not gain many votes by claiming credit for the 
  economic prosperity of any particular province when, on average, national 
  economic conditions are deteriorating. The results further suggest that 
  provincial incumbents are not held accountable for economic conditions in 
  their provinces, but are rather punished for national economic deterioration 
  when the incumbent federal party is of the same partisan family.
