  the theoretical and policy implications of the findings, and reflects on the 
  theory and evidence. The chapter ties the argument and the empirical evidence 
  to broader debates in international relations and development, offering new 
  ideas for how international institutions can contribute to the second 
  generation of economic reform in developing countries. One of the central 
  arguments in this chapter is that preferential trading agreements have 
  significant non-trade effects that should be considered in evaluating their 
  contribution to economic multilateralism.
