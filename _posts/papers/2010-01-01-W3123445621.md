abstract_fr: 'This article presents a case study of the recent merger between the
  Progressive-Conservative Party and the Reform/Canadian Alliance parties. The selection
  of this case serves to illustrate the current limits of existing party organisational
  change and party coalition theories when it comes to explaining party mergers. We
  propose an alternative theoretical framework that introduces some minor adjustments
  to the existing literature in order to account for the party merger phenomenon.
  In this framework, three factors are shown to be most likely to have led to a party
  merger in the Canadian context: Votes-seats disproportionality, access to new resources
  (electoral and financial) and rebranding. We conclude with a discussion regarding
  these factors’ potential for explaining other cases of party mergers.'
