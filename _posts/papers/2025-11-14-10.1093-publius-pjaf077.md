abstract_fr: Abstract Understanding the complex relationships between 
  territorial identity, federal institutions, and political attitudes in 
  multinational states requires accurately measuring territorial identity. 
  However, popular measures of territorial identity such as the Linz-Moreno 
  scale are increasingly contested, while newer alternatives are poorly 
  understood. This article provides theoretical guidance on how to select and 
  interpret different quantitative measures of territorial identity based on 
  evidence from an original survey in Quebec. Through survey experiments, 
  correlations with associated political attitudes, and quantitative text 
  analysis, we show that “relative” measures of identity, such as the 
  Linz-Moreno scale, are well-suited to capturing nationalist sentiment, while 
  “absolute” measures, such as point scales, are better at quantifying the 
  intensity of particular territorial attachments. Among the two point-based 
  measures we test, we find that a constrained allocation is better at revealing
  more politicized forms of Quebec identity.
