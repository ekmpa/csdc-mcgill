  intervention approaches and highlight the necessity of addressing 
  socioeconomic inequality as both a social justice and public health imperative
  in preventing problematic technology use. Child maltreatment represents a 
  significant risk factor for numerous adverse outcomes, yet its relationship 
  with problematic technology use (PTU) remains inadequately understood. 
  Existing meta-analytic studies have failed to distinguish between different 
  PTU subtypes and have not examined cross-national moderating factors. This 
  study employed a three-level random-effects meta-analysis to systematically 
  examine associations between child maltreatment and PTU, while exploring 
  specific patterns across different maltreatment types and PTU subtypes. 
  Following PRISMA 2020 guidelines, we conducted comprehensive database searches
  across Web of Science, MEDLINE, SCOPUS, PubMed, and PsycINFO. Studies were 
  assessed using the Mixed Methods Appraisal Tool, with a mean quality score of 
  3.82 (SD = 0.72). The final analysis included 98 studies comprising 260 effect
  sizes and 272,491 participants. Results revealed a significant positive 
  correlation between child maltreatment and PTU ( r = 0.200, 95% CI [0.175, 
  0.224]), representing a small-to-moderate effect size. Substantial 
  heterogeneity was observed ( I 2 = 97.7%), with within-study variance (68.83%)
  exceeding between-study variance (30.14%). Moderation analyses demonstrated 
  that emotional abuse exhibited the strongest association with PTU ( r = 
  0.233), followed by physical neglect ( r = 0.216). Gender composition, income 
  inequality, and survival/self-expression cultural values significantly 
  moderated the association. Measurement instruments also influenced effect 
  sizes, with standardized scales showing stronger associations than 
  self-reported usage time. These findings support theoretical frameworks 
  linking early trauma to subsequent maladaptive technology use while 
  highlighting the importance of considering maltreatment heterogeneity and 
  sociocultural contexts. Results have implications for trauma-informed 
  prevention and intervention strategies, emphasizing the need for targeted 
  approaches based on specific maltreatment-PTU combinations.
