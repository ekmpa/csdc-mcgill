abstract_fr: Recent research on the consequences of ethnic diversity for social 
  cohesion indicates that the effects of diversity are not necessarily 
  universal. In this article we hypothesise that the rhetoric of political 
  parties conditions whether diversity negatively affects generalised trust. 
  Political campaigns might highlight the salience of cultural diversity issues 
  in their discourse or, moreover, use a divisive rhetoric of ‘nationalistic’ 
  positions. Thus political mobilisation might heighten the perceived conflict 
  between those who are native born and immigrants, especially in diverse 
  societies. In order to test this argument, we investigate the influence of 
  political rhetoric framed on cultural diversity issues, that is, nationalism 
  and multiculturalism — obtained from the Comparative Manifestos Project– on 
  generalised trust in 21 European democracies. We find that the negative impact
  of ethnic diversity on trust is particularly strong when these issues are 
  mobilised by political parties. It does not, however, matter whether these 
  issues are presented in a positive or negative light.
