abstract_fr: Empathy is a cornerstone of high-quality surgical care, directly influencing
  patient satisfaction, treatment adherence, clinical outcomes, and medicolegal risk
  reduction. Despite its recognized importance, a growing body of evidence suggests
  that empathy significantly declines during surgical training. Surgical residents
  encounter a uniquely challenging environment characterized by burnout rates ranging
  from 50% to 70%, rigid hierarchical and emotionally suppressive training cultures,
  excessive work hours, frequent exposure to traumatic patient events, and severe
  time constraints that curtail meaningful patient-provider interactions. Critically,
  formal, structured training in empathic communication remains notably absent from
  most surgical curricula. While various studies have explored empathy within this
  population, the existing evidence base remains fragmented, heterogeneous, and disconnected
  across different surgical subspecialties and educational contexts. Consequently,
  there is a pressing need for a systematic synthesis of this evidence to consolidate
  current knowledge, clarify definitions, and direct future inquiry.
